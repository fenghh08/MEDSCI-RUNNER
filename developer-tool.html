Start here

The whole flow in four steps. Most people only ever need the first three — the sheet, paste it in, done. Nothing you add touches the game until an admin merges it, so there's no way to break anything.

1 · Get the template sheet

Work in Google Sheets — one row per question. Make a copy of the template so you have the exact column layout; there's a tab in it for questions and one for lectures. Type your name and email at the top of this page too — that's how your work gets credited, and how an admin can reach you about it.

📋 Open the template sheet ↗

2 · Fill it in

Questions: course code (e.g. MEDS3002), lecture (e.g. L14), topic, item name, the prompt, four options, and which is correct. If the item doesn't exist yet it's created for you. Lectures & learning outcomes: lecture number, name, and the outcomes separated by semicolons — one row per lecture, on the lectures tab of the sheet.

Topics and courses have to exist before a row can point at them. Topics you can add yourself under Add content; a brand-new course is admin-only — ask one of the admins and it takes a minute.

3 · Paste it into the tool

Select the filled-in rows in your sheet, copy, then click a cell in the table here and paste — it fills every cell you copied and grows the table if needed. Each row is checked as you go; anything missing gets a note and, where possible, a button straight to the fix.

4 · It goes to the team queue

Everything lands in Team queue, live, where the whole team can see it. An admin reviews and merges it into the game — usually in a batch. You can edit or fix your own submissions there until they're merged. Players' in-game reports about existing questions show up separately under Reports.

⚠️ Needs review 

        
Auto-surfaced: a high miss rate with enough attempts to trust it, or a difficulty tag that doesn't match how the question actually performs (e.g. tagged "easy" but missed most of the time). Flag one straight to the team's Reports queue, or jump in and fix it yourself.

        

      

      

        
📐 Question quality 

        
Uses each player's first attempt only. % correct is how hard the question is; D (discrimination index) is how much better the strongest players do than the weakest — the share of the top 27% (by their own accuracy on other questions) who got it right minus the share of the bottom 27%. A low D means the question isn't telling students apart, and the verdict separates the two reasons: too easy (nearly everyone is right, so D is naturally low) versus poorly written (mid-difficulty, yet strong players do no better than weak ones). D ≥ 0.40 excellent · 0.30–0.39 good · 0.20–0.29 fair · 0.10–0.19 marginal · under 0.10 poor · negative means check the marked answer.

        

          
        

        

      

      

        
By course

        
Sorted hardest first (lowest accuracy).

        

      

      

        
By topic

          
Every topic with at least one recorded answer, hardest first.

          

        

      

      

        
Hardest questions

        
Sorted by miss rate. Low-attempt questions are noisy (one wrong answer out of one attempt is a 100% miss rate) — raise the minimum to filter those out.

        

        

      

      

        
Easiest questions

          
Lowest miss rate with at least 3 attempts — the flip side of "hardest", mostly useful for spotting a question that's a giveaway rather than actually testing anything.
